Financial Metrics

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate

The rate at which a value (like revenue) grows per year on average over a period of time. It smooths out ups and downs to give a single consistent growth number.

Example: If revenue was $100M in 2021 and $173M in 2024, the 3-year CAGR is 20% — meaning it grew 20% per year on average.

EPS — Earnings Per Share

The amount of profit a company earns for each share of stock. Higher EPS generally means the company is more profitable.

Example: If a company earns $1 billion in profit and has 100 million shares, EPS = $10.00 per share.

P/E Ratio — Price to Earnings Ratio

How much investors are paying for each dollar of earnings. A high P/E means investors expect strong future growth. A low P/E may indicate the stock is undervalued.

Example: If a stock trades at $150 and EPS is $10, P/E = 15x. Investors pay $15 for every $1 of earnings.

FCF — Free Cash Flow

The cash a company generates after paying for its operations and capital expenditures. Positive FCF means the company has money left over to invest, pay dividends, or buy back stock.

Example: Operating Cash Flow of $10B minus Capital Expenditures of $3B = FCF of $7B.

Interest Coverage Ratio

How easily a company can pay its interest expenses using its operating income. A ratio above 3x is generally considered safe. Below 1x means the company cannot cover its interest.

Example: Operating Income of $5B divided by Interest Expense of $500M = Coverage of 10x. Very strong.

Market Cap — Market Capitalization

The total value of all a company's shares of stock. It represents what the market thinks the entire company is worth.

Example: 1 billion shares × $150 per share = $150 billion market cap.

Revenue

The total amount of money a company earns from selling its products or services before any expenses are deducted. Also called "top line" or "sales".

Example: Microsoft sells software, cloud services, and hardware. All those sales combined = total revenue.
SEC & Regulatory

10-K Filing

An annual report that every US public company must file with the SEC. It contains detailed financial statements, business description, risk factors, and management discussion.

Example: Microsoft files a 10-K every July covering its fiscal year ending June 30.

SEC — Securities and Exchange Commission

The US government agency that regulates the stock market and requires public companies to disclose financial information. All filings are publicly available on SEC EDGAR for free.

Example: You can read any company's 10-K at edgar.sec.gov at no cost.

SEC EDGAR

The SEC's free online database where all public company filings are stored and searchable. EDGAR stands for Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval.

Example: Our platform fetches 10-K filing URLs directly from SEC EDGAR using their free API.
Technical & Platform

yfinance

A free Python library that downloads financial data from Yahoo Finance. Used in this platform to fetch company info, revenue, cash flow, and balance sheet data.

Example: yf.Ticker("MSFT").financials returns Microsoft's income statement as a table.

JSON — JavaScript Object Notation

A simple text format for storing and exchanging data. This platform uses JSON files to store company scores, watchlists, and dashboard data.

Example: {"ticker": "MSFT", "score": 85.6} is a JSON object with two fields.

RSS Feed

A standardized format websites use to publish frequently updated content like news headlines. Our platform uses Yahoo Finance RSS feeds to show live news for each company.

Example: Yahoo Finance publishes an RSS feed for every stock ticker that updates with the latest news articles.

Flask

A lightweight Python web framework used in this platform to build the news proxy server. Flask listens for requests from the browser and fetches news data on its behalf.

Example: When the dashboard loads, it asks Flask at port 5000 for MSFT news. Flask fetches it from Yahoo and returns it.

CORS — Cross-Origin Resource Sharing

A browser security rule that prevents a webpage from fetching data from a different domain. Our Flask proxy server bypasses this by acting as a middleman.

Example: The dashboard at port 8080 cannot directly fetch from Yahoo Finance, so it asks our proxy at port 5000 instead.
